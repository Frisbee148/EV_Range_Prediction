1. Problem Statement, Project Aim, and End-Result Goal (PRD §1–§3)

1.1 Operational Problem

A standard data-driven neural network (Model A) learns statistical correlations between vehicle kinematics and battery energy consumption. However, a purely data-driven model does not enforce physical conservation laws between its prediction heads. When a neural network predicts State of Charge (SOC_hat[t]), Battery Terminal Power (P_hat[t]), and Remaining Driving Range (R_hat[t]) through separate output heads, three physical consistency failures occur:

  • Energy-Integration Violation: The predicted step-to-step SOC change (SOC_hat[t+1] - SOC_hat[t]) does not equal the discrete time integral of the model's own predicted battery power (-P_hat[t] * dt / E_usable).
  • Boundary Violation: The model can output SOC values below 0.0 (0%) or above 1.0 (100%), or negative remaining driving range (R_hat < 0 m).
  • Thermodynamic Monotonicity Violation: The model can predict an increasing SOC (SOC_hat[t+1] > SOC_hat[t]) during timesteps where the model simultaneously predicts positive battery discharge power (P_hat[t] > 0 W).

1.2 Project Aim and Research Questions

The aim of this project is to design, train, and evaluate a multi-vehicle Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN, Model B) built on a 2-layer Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) sequence backbone. The training objective combines supervised label error (L_data) with a label-free self-consistency physics penalty (L_physics):

(L6) L_total = L_data + lambda(epoch) * L_physics

Unlike the degenerate v1 formulation (which compared predicted power against deterministic physics labels and therefore duplicated L_data), this project defines L_physics exclusively over the model's own de-normalised predictions. The project answers four core research questions:

  • RQ1 (Primary — Information Gaps): Under sparse SOC supervision (E3), sensor noise (E4), or limited training data (E2), does a physics-consistency-regularised LSTM predict EV remaining range, SOC, and power more accurately and more physically consistently than an identically configured data-only LSTM?
  • RQ2 (Zero-Shot Cross-Vehicle Generalisation — E5): Does physics regularisation improve zero-shot extrapolation to unseen vehicle configurations in a 4-fold Leave-One-Vehicle-Out (LOVO) protocol?
  • RQ3 (Cross-Regime Generalisation — E6): Does physics regularisation improve extrapolation when the model trains on Urban and Arterial traffic and tests on unseen Highway traffic where aerodynamic drag (v^2) dominates?
  • RQ4 (Data-Ablation Crossover — E2): How much training data does the data-only LSTM require to match the accuracy and physical consistency of the physics-informed LSTM across {5%, 10%, 25%, 50%, 100%} trip subsets?
End-Result Goal Achieved: All nine PRD §16 experiments (E1 through E9, comprising 196 neural training runs and 392 saved artifacts) were executed under strict matched-budget controls on an NVIDIA Tesla T4 GPU. Model B reduces energy-balance residual RMS by 46% in-distribution (E1), by over 3.1× under sparse SOC supervision (E3, k=300), improves unseen Highway range MAE by 1.86 km (E6), and improves Range MAE by 8.23 km under 10-second BMS SOC reporting (E3, k=10), while maintaining 0.00% SOC bound violations across every experiment.

4. How SUMO & the Longitudinal Physics Engine Work (PRD §6, §7, §9)

4.1 SUMO MoST 3D Network Extraction & IDM Car-Following Kinematics

A critical failure mode in EV simulation is using a flat road network where elevation z = 0 everywhere, which turns the gravitational grade term F_grade = m * g * sin(theta) into dead code (PRD §9.2). Audit of the legacy network (map_with_tls.net.xml) and the Luxembourg network (lust.net.xml) showed zero elevation coordinates. Therefore, this project uses the Monaco SUMO Traffic (MoST) network (most.net.xml), which contains true 3D road geometry spanning -0.08 m to 616.28 m above sea level.

  1. 3D Edge Parsing (src/simulation/most_net.py): The parser extracts all non-internal road edges, lane speed limits (speed_mps), lengths, successor connectivity graphs, and 3D polyline vertices (x, y, z).
  2. Scenario Stratification: Edges are partitioned by speed limit into three driving regimes:
    • Urban: speed_mps ≤ 14.0 m/s (≤ 50.4 km/h), stop gap 40–90 s, dwell time 8–18 s, traffic factor 0.12–1.0.
    • Arterial: 14.0 < speed_mps ≤ 22.0 m/s (50.4–79.2 km/h), stop gap 90–180 s, dwell time 4–10 s, traffic factor 0.35–1.0.
    • Highway: speed_mps > 22.0 m/s (> 79.2 km/h), stop gap 250–450 s, dwell time 0–3 s, traffic factor 0.45–1.0.
  3. Trajectory Diversity & IDM Kinematics (src/simulation/idm_trace.py): For each of the 4 vehicles and 3 scenarios, 20 distinct connected walks are sampled using vehicle- and scenario-specific random seeds (240 trips × 600 s = 144,000 rows at dt = 1.0 s). Speed evolves according to the Intelligent Driver Model (IDM) free-road/congested acceleration law a = a_max * (1 - (v / v_cap)^4) with per-vehicle acceleration caps (a_max), braking limits (b_dec), and driver imperfection noise (sigma).
  4. Verification Gates Passed: Across the 144,000 generated rows, elevation standard deviation is 170.88 m (exceeding the PRD gate std(z) > 1.0 m by 170×), and maximum pairwise cross-vehicle speed correlation is 0.331 (well below the PRD gate |corr| < 0.90).

4.2 Exact Longitudinal Vehicle Dynamics & Range Equations (E1–E14)

All physics modules in src/physics/ compute strictly in SI base units (m, s, kg, W, J, rad):

(E1) F_aero[t] = 0.5 * rho * C_d * A * v[t]^2 (E2) F_roll[t] = m * g * C_rr * cos(theta[t]) (E3) F_grade[t] = m * g * sin(theta[t]) (E4) F_accel[t] = lambda_rot * m * a[t] (E5) F_trac[t] = F_aero[t] + F_roll[t] + F_grade[t] + F_accel[t] (E6) P_wheel[t] = F_trac[t] * v[t] (E7) P_batt[t] = P_wheel[t] / eta_dt + P_aux[t] when P_wheel[t] >= 0 (Propulsion) (E8) P_batt[t] = max( P_wheel[t] * eta_rg , -P_regen_max ) + P_aux[t] when P_wheel[t] < 0 and v[t] >= 5.0 m/s (E9) P_batt[t] = P_aux[t] when P_wheel[t] < 0 and v[t] < 5.0 m/s (E10) P_aux[t] = P_aux_base + k_hvac * abs(T_amb[t] - T_comfort) [T_comfort = 21 C, k_hvac = 60 W/C] (E11) SOC[t+1] = clip( SOC[t] - P_batt[t] * dt / E_usable , 0.0 , 1.0 ) (E12) E_rem[t] = SOC[t] * E_usable (E13) R_A[t] = E_rem[t] / max( EMA_300s( P_batt[t] / max(v[t], 0.5) ) , 1e-6 ) [masked until distance >= 500 m] (E14) R_B[t] = CX[n*] - CX[t], where n* = min{ n > t : CE[n] - CE[t] >= E_rem[t] } [Primary Oracle Label]
Real-World Physics Validation Gate (TUM BMW i3 TripA01 — docs/physics_tum_validation.md): Running equations (E1)–(E11) on 1,008.9 seconds (10,090 samples at 10 Hz) of real Munich driving telemetry with std(z) = 5.58 m yielded a simulated end-of-trip SOC of 0.819 against the measured BMW BMS end SOC of 0.815 (an error of only 0.4 percentage points), and simulated trip energy within 7.4% of SOC-implied energy (0.941 kWh vs. 1.015 kWh).

6. Neural Network Architecture & Self-Consistency Loss (PRD §12–§15)

Figure 2 illustrates the multi-head sequence architecture implemented in src/models/lstm.py and src/losses/physics_loss.py. At each timestep t within a window of length T_seq = 60 (stride S = 5), the 7 normalised dynamic features are concatenated with the 9 normalised static vehicle parameters, projected through a Linear(16 → 128) + ReLU layer, and processed by a 2-layer LSTM (hidden_dim = 128, dropout = 0.1). Three linear heads emit normalised predictions at every timestep, which are immediately de-normalised using the training split's StandardScaler statistics before entering the physics residual.

8. Known Limitations, Definition-of-Done Audit & Conclusions (PRD §23–§26)

8.1 Synthesis of Findings & Key Architectural Insights

Key Discussion Takeaways & PINN Behavior:
  • Why Model A and Model B Achieve Comparable Range MAE on Clean In-Distribution Data (PRD §3.1): The training dataset was generated deterministically by the exact longitudinal differential equations (E1–E14). Because there are no missing physics or unmodeled real-world disturbances in the clean synthetic data, an expressive sequence model (Model A) trained with supervised labels (soc, p_batt_w, range_b_m) learns the underlying physical equations directly from data.
  • Coupling of the Loss Terms: The physics consistency term (L2) directly constrains the relationship between predicted SOC step changes and battery power: SOC[t+1] - (SOC[t] - P_batt[t]*dt/E_usable). Notice that (L2) does not directly contain the predicted range label R[t]. As a consequence, Model B produces dramatic improvements in Power prediction (-56% MAE at 5% data) and electrochemical consistency (-25% to -50% residual RMS), while Range MAE benefits primarily when context length is expanded (E8, 120s sequence length) or when explicit range consistency (L4) is enabled.
  • Role of the Datasets:
    • Argonne National Lab (D3): Provided real-world dynamometer test-bench data across 8 production EVs, supplying the coastdown coefficients (A, B, C), rolling resistance, aerodynamic drag areas, and auxiliary load baselines used to configure each vehicle in configs/vehicles.yaml.
    • Monaco SUMO Traffic (MoST): Solved the zero-grade flat dyno limitation by providing real 3D mountain topography (z ∈ [-0.08, 616.3] m, std(z) = 170.88 m) and realistic microscopic urban/arterial/highway traffic kinematics.
    • TUM BMW i3: Provided real on-road driving telemetry around Munich to validate the physics engine, achieving end-of-trip SOC agreement within 0.4 percentage points on TripA01.

8.2 Known Limitations (Stated Without Hedging per PRD §23)

  1. Simulated Training Trajectories: All 144,000 rows in sumo_most_1hz.csv.gz are generated by IDM car-following kinematics over MoST 3D road geometry and labeled by the project's physics engine. While the physics engine is validated within 7.4% of SOC-implied energy on real TUM BMW i3 telemetry, neural training results measure controlled simulation performance.
  2. Shared Physics Generator and Regulariser: Ground-truth labels (soc, p_batt_w, range_b_m) are produced by the same discrete energy-balance law that (L2) enforces. Consequently, on 100% clean, densely labelled data (E1), Model A and Model B achieve statistically equivalent Range MAE; Model B's accuracy advantage emerges specifically when information gaps (E3 k=10, E4 noise, E6 highway shift, E2 5–50% data) are present.
  3. Constant Efficiencies & No Electrochemistry: Drivetrain efficiency (η_dt) and regenerative efficiency (η_rg) are modelled as speed- and load-independent constants, and internal battery resistance, cell thermal dynamics, and state-of-health ageing are omitted.
  4. Cycle-Repetition Assumption in Range Label B: Primary label range_b_m (E14) assumes the current 600-second trip repeats cyclically until battery depletion, matching EPA/WLTP certification conventions rather than route-ahead navigation lookahead.
